The FY2026 budget is balanced and auditable. The FY2027 proposal is nearly flat, but “flat” conceals large capital reallocations, Plant Vogtle valuation exposure, employee-cost pressure, and a document-control failure that prevents the latest cuts from being traced account by account.

What is working

  • FY2026 revenues and expenditures both equal $95,213,210.
  • Staff eliminated request-stage gaps in both budget cycles.
  • The latest FY2027 proposal is essentially flat and the General Fund declines 0.93%.
  • The June FY2027 draft removes a $705K General Fund balance draw.
  • A $6.542M road package is 77.9% grant-funded, with a $1.448M local share.

Pressure points

  • Utility-tax lines provide $40.6M—42.6% of the FY2026 all-funds total.
  • FY2027 carries a 2% COLA, funded steps, and a 12% health-premium increase.
  • The presentation's 2% COLA trails its own 3.6% South-region CPI evidence by 1.6 points.
  • The June draft still uses $8.69M of balance forward in other funds.
  • Fuel is held flat based partly on an uncertain geopolitical assumption.

Control gaps

  • June/July FY2027 documents total $96.071M; the August summary totals $95.240M.
  • No updated itemized file explains the $830,910 reduction.
  • The Munis percentage column usually measures request growth, not the adjacent recommendation/approval.
  • The $6.1M detention-renovation headline cannot be cleanly reconciled without eliminating transfers.

Plant Vogtle / taxes

The county cites a $2.8B assessed-value reduction and proposes roughly $800K of cuts to avoid a 0.10-mill increase. The later total is $830,910 lower, which is consistent with that response.

Forward readout

The next cycle is likely to be defined by Vogtle valuation pressure, employee-cost growth, rotating capital priorities, and increased scrutiny of late-stage revisions. A tax increase is possible but not predetermined.
